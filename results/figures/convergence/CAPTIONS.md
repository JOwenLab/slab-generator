# Figure captions

Generated by `plot_convergence.py`. Every number is derived from the
source CSVs and run directories rather than transcribed.

Sign convention (CLAUDE.md section 2): positive sigma means the cell is
COMPRESSED and wants to expand; negative sigma means it is in TENSION.
All slabs are symmetric H/H terminated, 90/720 Ry unless stated.

## Figure 1 - Plane-wave cutoff convergence

Plane-wave cutoff convergence of the symmetric H-terminated diamond slabs, both panels plotted as a deviation from the same 120 Ry reference and on a common y-scale so the two are directly comparable. (a) The mean in-plane stress drifts by 2.2-2.8 kbar between 60 and 120 Ry, in the same direction on every surface: a residual Pulay contribution to the stress rather than any change in the surface. (b) The in-plane anisotropy, on the identical ruler, never leaves 0.07 kbar of its converged value, so anisotropy-derived quantities are already converged at 60 Ry while the mean stress is not -- the difference is visible rather than asserted. 90 Ry (dashed) is adopted for production, where the residual is 0.07 kbar in the mean stress and 0.01 kbar in the anisotropy, both far below the surface-to-surface differences the campaign is measuring. Positive sigma means the cell is compressed.

(100) The production 5x5x1 mesh (dashed, 25.3 A) sits within 0.005 kbar of the densest mesh computed (50.5 A). (110) The production 9x7x1 mesh (dashed, 22.7 A) sits within 0.010 kbar of the densest mesh computed (37.9 A). ## Figure 3 - Vacuum convergence: sigma versus tau

Vacuum convergence of the symmetric H-terminated 6-layer slabs, and the reason surface stress is reported as tau rather than sigma. The raw QE stress (dashed, left axis) is averaged over the whole supercell including the vacuum, so it falls by a factor of 2.1 as the vacuum grows from 8 to 24 A while nothing physical changes. The vacuum-corrected tau = sigma*Lz/2 (solid, right axis) moves by at most 0.0024 N/m across that same range, non-monotonically, which is numerical noise rather than drift. tau is therefore the transferable quantity and 8 A of vacuum already suffices for it; the apparent sigma convergence problem is an artefact of dividing by an arbitrary cell height, not an unconverged calculation.

Files: `fig3_vacuum.pdf`, `fig3_vacuum.png` (600 dpi), `fig3_vacuum_annotated.pdf`

## Figure 4 - Slab-thickness ladder

Slab-thickness ladder at both lattice constants. Plotting sigma_mean*Lz against the bulk-equivalent thickness t = N_C*Omega_bulk/A splits the total in-plane force per unit length into a thickness-independent intercept, twice the surface stress tau_inf, and a slope sigma_res carried by the slab interior. t is atom-counted rather than measured as a carbon z-extent because the z-extent depends on where one declares the slab to end: refitting these same points against the z-extent moves tau_inf by up to 0.6% here, and up to 4.3% on the superseded ladder, where the larger interior stress amplifies it. Rebuilding the ladder at the corrected a0 = 3.572997 A (filled, solid) collapses the slope from -2.75..-2.46 kbar at the superseded a0 = 3.573641 A (open, dashed) to -0.36..-0.26 kbar, while tau_inf moves by at most 2.0%. The interior stress was therefore an artefact of the reference lattice constant and not a property of any surface, and removing it left the surface stress intact -- which is what licenses the corrected ladder as the production result. The 6L point (struck through) lies outside the asymptotic regime and is excluded from both fits but still plotted. Each surface keeps its own y-scale because the three sit ~1000 kbar A apart. Positive sigma means the cell is compressed; tau inherits that sign, so positive tau_inf is a compressive surface stress (minus the continuum f). Fits are read from fit_tau_infinity.py, not recomputed here.

Files: `fig4_thickness.pdf`, `fig4_thickness.png` (600 dpi), `fig4_thickness_annotated.pdf`

## Figure 5 - Bulk equation of state

Bulk diamond equation of state at 90/720 Ry, and the bias in a linear P(eps) fit. (a) P(eps) is visibly curved over +/-1% strain, so the straight-line fit misses the zero-pressure crossing: the two markers on P = 0 differ by 0.0283% in strain, which is 0.0010 A in a0. (b) The residuals make the bias unmistakable: the linear fit leaves 3.33 kbar rms with obvious systematic curvature, 37 times the 0.089 kbar of the quadratic. The production reference is neither of the fits drawn here but a 3rd-order Birch-Murnaghan fit to E(V) over the same series, giving a0 = 3.572997 A and B = 433.66 GPa; the quadratic P(eps) fit shown in (a) is its cross-check, not its source.
